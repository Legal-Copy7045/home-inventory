Read an equipment manual into a maintenance plan, list the parts each service needs, and see whether you have them. Any brand that meets the grade counts.

**Updating:**
1. Update the App and the integration in HACS. The App upgrades its database automatically; it only adds tables and columns, so nothing existing changes.
2. Reading a manual needs an AI provider configured in the App.

## New
- **Read the manual.** On equipment you track, tap **Read the manual** and choose its PDF, or photos of the maintenance pages. The AI finds:
  - each service and its interval (e.g. oil every 50 hours or yearly, first at 5 hours);
  - what that particular service needs (the 100-hour service can need a plug and an air filter that the 50-hour one doesn't);
  - the parts and consumables, with part numbers and grades exactly as printed and their page numbers.

  You check and edit it all before anything is saved. Existing tasks are updated, not duplicated, and parts you already have are matched by part number.
- **Parts on each maintenance task**, each showing *In stock*, *Not enough*, *None in stock* or *Check*. The model remembers what it takes.
- **Standards (any brand).** "Engine oil SAE 10W-30" accepts any product you've confirmed as meeting it. Products whose specifications match are *suggested*, and you confirm them.
  - A task that names a brand can use equivalents, unless you untick that (e.g. for a warranty).
  - Replaced part numbers count one step only.
- **Parts needed** on the Maintenance page adds up what's due across all your equipment (shared oil is counted once), with one tap to put shortfalls on the shopping list.
- **Done on a service** fills in the parts from stock. Pieces are taken out; tick *used up* when a bottle is empty.

## Notes
- Nothing counts as equivalent until you confirm it, and part numbers are never guessed.
- Reading a manual sends its text (only the maintenance pages, for long manuals) and the equipment's make and model to your AI provider. Serial numbers and owner details are never sent.
